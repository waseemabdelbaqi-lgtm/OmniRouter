## Chapter 2 — The Atmosphere

:::sol SG-C2-E001
**(a) 420 ppm. (b) 0.101 m³. (c) 187.4 m³. (d) 50%. (e) Water vapor.**
(a) ppm = percent × 10,000 = 0.0420 × 10,000 = 420 ppm.
(b) Volume = 240 m³ × (0.0420 ÷ 100) = 240 × 0.000420 = 0.1008 m³ ≈ 0.101 m³.
(c) Volume = 240 m³ × (78.08 ÷ 100) = 240 × 0.7808 = 187.39 m³ ≈ 187.4 m³.
(d) Percent increase = (420 − 280) ÷ 280 × 100 = 140 ÷ 280 × 100 = 50%.
(e) Water vapor varies from near 0% to about 4% with temperature and place; N₂ and CO₂ are nearly constant everywhere.
:::

:::sol SG-C2-E002
**(a) City P 923 mm, City Q 595 mm. (b) City P 10°C, City Q 34°C. (c) 36%. (d) City P is more likely coastal. (e) Dry summer.**
(a) Sum of the 12 months. P: 150 + 120 + 100 + 70 + 45 + 20 + 8 + 10 + 30 + 80 + 130 + 160 = 923 mm. Q: 30 + 30 + 40 + 45 + 60 + 75 + 80 + 70 + 55 + 45 + 35 + 30 = 595 mm.
(b) Range = warmest − coldest. P: 19 − 9 = 10°C. Q: 22 − (−12) = 34°C.
(c) Percent less = (P − Q) ÷ P × 100 = (923 − 595) ÷ 923 × 100 = 328 ÷ 923 × 100 = 35.5% ≈ 36%.
(d) City P has the much smaller temperature range (10°C versus 34°C). Water heats and cools slowly, so coastal climates are mild; City Q's large range indicates an inland (continental) climate.
(e) City P has a dry summer: precipitation is under 50 mm from May through September, lowest in July (8 mm) and August (10 mm), with a wet winter.
:::

:::sol SG-C2-E003
**(a) 115 mph. (b) Category 3. (c) 30 hours. (d) The storm loses its warm-water energy source and friction slows its winds.**
(a) 185 km/h ÷ 1.609 km/h per mph = 114.98 ≈ 115 mph.
(b) 115 mph lies in the 111–129 mph range, which is Category 3 (178–208 km/h; 185 km/h agrees).
(c) Time = distance ÷ speed = 420 km ÷ 14 km/h = 30 h.
(d) A hurricane draws heat and water vapor (latent heat) from warm ocean water. Over land that supply is cut off and friction with the surface slows the winds, so the storm weakens.
:::
