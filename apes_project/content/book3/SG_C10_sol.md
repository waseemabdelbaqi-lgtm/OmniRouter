## Chapter 10 — Impacts on the Environment and Human Health

:::sol SG-C10-E001
**(a) 525.6 kg per year. (b) 17.5%. (c) see below.**
(a) Volume per year = 25 L/min × 60 min/h × 24 h/day × 365 days = 25 × 525,600 = 13,140,000 L. Mass = volume × concentration = 13,140,000 L × 40 mg/L = 525,600,000 mg. Convert: 525,600,000 mg ÷ 1,000,000 = **525.6 kg** (check: 13,140,000 × 40 = 525,600,000).
(b) Percent removed = 525.6 ÷ 3,000 × 100 = **17.5%** (525.6 ÷ 3,000 = 0.1752).
(c) The concentration in the pumped water falls over time, because contaminant that is stuck to soil and rock is released only slowly (and the plume keeps moving), so less benzene is removed per liter in later years. The simple division (3,000 ÷ 525.6 ≈ 5.7 years) assumes a constant 40 mg/L and therefore underestimates the time.
:::

:::sol SG-C10-E002
**(a) 80 units. (b) 199 years. (c) see below.**
(a) Number of half-lives n = 90 ÷ 30 = 3. Remaining = 640 × (½)³ = 640 ÷ 8 = **80 units** (640 → 320 → 160 → 80).
(b) Set (½)ⁿ = 0.01, so n = log(100) ÷ log(2) = 2 ÷ 0.30103 = 6.644 half-lives. Time = 6.644 × 30 yr = 199.3, so about **199 years** (check: 0.5⁶·⁶⁴⁴ = 0.0100).
(c) Waste stays hazardous for roughly ten half-lives. For cesium-137 that is about 300 years; for plutonium-239 it is about 10 × 24,100 = 241,000 years, so the repository must keep the waste isolated roughly 800 times longer.
:::

:::sol SG-C10-E003
**(a) Q = 40 thousand units, P = $60. (b) Q = 30 thousand units. (c) $20 per unit. (d) $600 thousand.**
(a) Demand = private cost: 100 − Q = 20 + Q → 80 = 2Q → Q = 40. Price P = 100 − 40 = **$60** (check: 20 + 40 = 60).
(b) Social cost = 20 + Q + 20 = 40 + Q. Set 100 − Q = 40 + Q → 60 = 2Q → **Q = 30** thousand units (price paid by buyers = 100 − 30 = $70).
(c) The tax equals the external cost, **$20 per unit**; the factory's cost becomes 20 + Q + 20 = 40 + Q, which gives Q = 30.
(d) Revenue = $20 per unit × 30 thousand units = **$600 thousand** per year.
:::

:::sol SG-C10-E004
**(a) $456,000. (b) 1.14. (c) 0.65; no. (d) see below.**
(a) PV = FV ÷ (1 + r)ᵗ = 1,000,000 ÷ 1.04²⁰. 1.04²⁰ = 2.1911, so PV = 1,000,000 ÷ 2.1911 = 456,387 ≈ **$456,000**.
(b) Benefit-cost ratio = 456,387 ÷ 400,000 = **1.14** (also 456,000 ÷ 400,000 = 1.14).
(c) At 7%: 1.07²⁰ = 3.8697, so PV = 1,000,000 ÷ 3.8697 = 258,419. Ratio = 258,419 ÷ 400,000 = **0.65**. This is below 1, so the discounted benefits no longer exceed the costs and the project **fails** the test at 7%.
(d) A higher discount rate shrinks the present value of benefits that arrive far in the future, so projects with long-delayed benefits (restoration, climate protection) look less worthwhile compared with projects that pay off quickly.
:::

:::sol SG-C10-E005
**(a) 9,600 m³ per year. (b) 14.6%. (c) see below. (d) see below.**
(a) Sustainable yield = area × growth rate = 1,200 ha × 8 m³/ha/yr = **9,600 m³ per year**.
(b) Overharvest = (11,000 − 9,600) ÷ 9,600 × 100 = 1,400 ÷ 9,600 × 100 = **14.6%** (0.14583).
(c) Resource depletion: the standing volume of wood (forest stock) would fall year after year. Biological diversity could also decline as habitat is lost; either answer is acceptable if the direction (decrease) is stated.
(d) Reduce the harvest to no more than 9,600 m³ per year (a cut of 1,400 m³ per year), or expand the area managed and replant so that growth matches the harvest.
:::
