## Chapter 4 — Ecosystems

:::sol SG-C4-E001
**(a) 2.0 × 10¹⁰ kcal/yr; (b) 2.0 × 10⁹ kcal/yr; (c) 2.0 × 10⁸ kcal/yr.**
(a) Total NPP = NPP per unit area × area. Convert the area: 25 km² × 10⁶ m²/km² = 2.5 × 10⁷ m². Total NPP = 800 kcal/m²/yr × 2.5 × 10⁷ m² = 2.0 × 10¹⁰ kcal/yr.
(b) NPP is the energy available to consumers, and 10% passes to the primary consumers: 2.0 × 10¹⁰ × 0.10 = 2.0 × 10⁹ kcal/yr.
(c) One more transfer: 2.0 × 10⁹ × 0.10 = 2.0 × 10⁸ kcal/yr (equivalently 2.0 × 10¹⁰ × 0.10² = 2.0 × 10⁸).
:::

:::sol SG-C4-E002
**(a) Richness is 5 before and 3 after; (b) −40%; (c) −45%; (d) richness counts kinds of species, while the number of individuals counts how many organisms there are.**
(a) Before: oak, maple, birch, hickory and beech = 5 species. After: birch and beech have 0 trees, so oak, maple and hickory remain = 3 species.
(b) Percent change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100 = (3 − 5) ÷ 5 × 100 = −40%, a 40% decrease in richness.
(c) Total trees before = 12 + 7 + 3 + 5 + 2 = 29. After = 9 + 4 + 0 + 3 + 0 = 16. Percent change = (16 − 29) ÷ 29 × 100 = −13 ÷ 29 × 100 = −44.8%, or about −45%.
(d) Richness ignores how many individuals each species has, so a plot of 500 trees of one species has richness 1, while a plot of 20 trees of five species has richness 5. Individual counts measure abundance, not variety. Here richness fell 40% and abundance fell about 45%, but the two measures can change independently.
:::
