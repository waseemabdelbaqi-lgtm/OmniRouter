## Unit 7 — Atmospheric Pollution

:::sol SG-U7-E001
**Answer:** (1) primary, (2) secondary, (3) primary, (4) secondary, (5) primary; carbon monoxide is the asphyxiant.

(a) Carbon monoxide leaves the tailpipe as CO, so it is **primary**. Ground-level ozone forms in the air from NOₓ and VOCs in sunlight, so it is **secondary**. Sulfur dioxide leaves the stack as SO₂, so it is **primary**. Nitric acid in rainwater forms when NOₓ reacts with oxygen and water in the air, so it is **secondary**. Soot is emitted directly by the diesel engine, so it is **primary**.

(b) Ozone: nitrogen oxides (NO/NO₂) or VOCs (either is accepted; both are needed with sunlight). Nitric acid: nitrogen oxides (NOₓ) from vehicles or power plants.

(c) The asphyxiant is **carbon monoxide (1)**: it binds to hemoglobin much more strongly than oxygen does, so the blood cannot carry enough oxygen to the tissues. A common wrong answer is soot, which is a particulate that damages the lungs but does not block oxygen transport.
:::

:::sol SG-U7-E002
**Answer:** (a) NO₂ peaks at 8:00; ozone peaks at 14:00. (b) 6 hours. (c) Ozone is a secondary pollutant that needs time and sunlight to form. (d) Ozone would be lower and flatter.

(a) NO₂ is highest at 8:00 (40 ppb). Ozone is highest at 14:00 (95 ppb). The smaller NO₂ rise at 18:00 (30 ppb) is the evening rush and is lower than the morning peak.

(b) Time between peaks = 14:00 − 8:00 = **6 hours**.

(c) Morning traffic releases NO, which becomes NO₂, together with VOCs. Sunlight splits NO₂ into NO and an oxygen atom, and the oxygen atom joins O₂ to make O₃. VOCs convert NO back to NO₂ without using up ozone, so the cycle repeats and ozone accumulates. Because the reactions take hours and need rising sunlight and temperature, ozone builds through the morning and peaks in the afternoon, after NO₂ has already been consumed and has fallen.

(d) On a cool, cloudy day the ozone curve would be **lower and flatter**, with a smaller peak (and possibly less accumulation overall). The traffic and NOₓ are the same, but less sunlight and lower temperature slow the reactions that make ozone, so fewer secondary pollutants form. NO₂ would stay high or fall more slowly because less of it is split by light.
:::

:::sol SG-U7-E003
**Answer:** (a) Day Y, from 0 to 500 m. (b) +6 °C. (c) Day Y had 55 µg/m³; Day X had 15 µg/m³.

(a) On Day X the temperature falls steadily with altitude (14 → 12 → 10 → 8 °C), which is the normal pattern. On Day Y the temperature **rises** from 6 °C at 0 m to 9 °C at 250 m and 12 °C at 500 m, so the inversion layer extends from **0 m to 500 m**. Above 500 m it falls again (11 °C at 750 m), so normal mixing resumes above the warm layer.

(b) Change = 12 °C − 6 °C = **+6 °C** (a rise of 6 °C over 500 m).

(c) Day Y (inversion) had the higher value, **55 µg/m³**; Day X had **15 µg/m³**. On Day Y the cold, dense surface air is capped by warmer air, so it cannot rise and mix. Fine particles from vehicles, heating and industry are trapped in the lowest 500 m and build up. On Day X warm surface air rises, mixing and diluting the particles.
:::

:::sol SG-U7-E011
**Answer:** model design below; a downwind rise alone does not prove the quarry is the cause.

(a) *If the quarry raises PM10, then PM10 will be higher at the monitor 1 km east (downwind) than at a monitor upwind of the quarry.*

(b) Independent variable: monitor location (downwind of the quarry versus upwind/background site). Dependent variable: PM10 concentration (µg/m³). Controlled variables (any two): same type of instrument, same height above the ground, same hours and days of measurement, same averaging time, similar distance from roads.

(c) Place one monitor east (downwind) at about 1 km from the quarry and one **upwind** to the west of the quarry (and optionally more at several distances). Measure at the same times over many days, covering different weather and both working and non-working days of the quarry. The upwind site measures the background PM10 that is present anyway (dust, traffic, pollen). Quarry contribution ≈ downwind reading − upwind reading.

(d) A higher reading east of the quarry is only a correlation. Something else could differ between the sites: a road, a construction site, a farm field or bare soil near the east monitor, wind changing direction, or a different elevation. Without an upwind site, many days of replicated data and control of such confounding variables, the data are consistent with the quarry as the cause but do not prove it.
:::
