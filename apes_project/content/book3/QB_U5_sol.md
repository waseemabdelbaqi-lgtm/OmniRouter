:::sol QB-U5-MCQ023

:::sol QB-U5-MCQ024

(a) Overgrazing (stocking beyond the land's ability to regenerate vegetation) leading to reduced plant cover, soil erosion, compaction, and risk of desertification. *(1 point: overgrazing, or erosion/desertification caused by it)*

(b) Example: "If cattle are grazed in rotation among paddocks, then plant cover (percent) at the end of the season will be higher than in continuously grazed paddocks." Equivalent acceptable: soil loss or bare ground will be lower under rotation. *(1 point: testable, compares the two systems, and states a direction)*

(c) *(1 point each)* (i) Independent variable: grazing system (continuous vs. rotational). (ii) Dependent variable, any one: percent plant cover, plant biomass (g/m²), amount of bare ground, soil compaction, sediment in the creek, species richness. (iii) Two controlled variables (both required for the point), any two: stocking density (2 cattle/ha), paddock size, soil type, slope, starting vegetation, rainfall, grazing period length, cattle breed. *(Point for (iii) only if both are correct. Stocking density per hectare is a strong answer because the herd sizes differ.)*

(d) Place randomly located quadrats (for example, 1 m² frames) in each paddock, estimate or count percent cover in each (or clip and weigh biomass), and average the results per paddock. Repeat at the start and at set intervals (for example, monthly through the grazing season and again in the following year). *(1 point: a specific sampling method with replicated or random sampling; 1 point: repeated measurement over time, with a stated frequency)*

(e) The three rotational paddocks have higher average plant cover (or lower bare ground) than the three continuous paddocks, and the difference is larger than the variation among paddocks within each group. *(1 point: higher cover or lower bare ground/erosion under rotation, consistent with the hypothesis)*

(f) Measure turbidity or total suspended solids (or sediment deposited) in the creek beside the paddocks after storms; expect lower turbidity or sediment downstream of the rotationally grazed paddocks. *(1 point: names a creek measurement and the expected lower value for rotation)*

(g) Any one solution with an advantage: reseed with native grasses (rapid cover, holds soil); build contour trenches or small check dams in gullies (slow runoff and trap sediment); fence the creek bank with a vegetated buffer strip (filters runoff, reduces sediment); reduce herd size (fewer animals matches the land's carrying capacity). *(1 point: a suitable solution with a matching advantage)*
