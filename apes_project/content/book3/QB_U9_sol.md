(ii) Any two of: water temperature, salinity, food (algae) type and amount, dissolved oxygen, light, tank size, larval density, age of larvae, parent stock. *(1 point: two valid controlled variables.)*

Use at least three replicate tanks per level, each with the same number of larvae (for example 200), start them at the same time, and count survivors at day 14. *(2 points: 1 for at least two pH levels with a control at the baseline; 1 for replicate tanks or repeated trials.)*

(f) Survival would decrease progressively as pH decreases, with the lowest survival in the lowest-pH tanks and the highest survival in the control. *(1 point: describes a trend consistent with the hypothesis written in part (c).)*

(g) Example solutions: stop pumping water during low-pH periods and store higher-pH water in advance; add a base such as sodium carbonate to buffer the water; move the intake to a different location; use water monitoring to time spawning when pH is high; support policies that reduce CO₂ emissions. Disadvantages: added cost or energy, buffer chemicals might change salinity or water chemistry, storage tanks can allow disease to grow, shutting the pumps reduces production in summer, emission policies take decades. *(2 points: 1 for a reasonable solution; 1 for a valid disadvantage or unintended consequence of that solution.)*
