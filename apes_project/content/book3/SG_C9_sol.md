## Chapter 9 — Pollution

:::sol SG-C9-E001
**(a) 82 ppb. (b) 17%. (c) NOₓ and VOCs; ozone is a secondary pollutant.**
(a) 1 ppm = 1000 ppb, so 0.082 ppm × 1000 = 82 ppb.
(b) Percent above limit = (82 − 70) ÷ 70 × 100 = 12 ÷ 70 × 100 = 17.1%, which rounds to 17%.
(c) Ozone forms in the air from nitrogen oxides and volatile organic compounds reacting in sunlight, so it is secondary.
:::

:::sol SG-C9-E002
**(a) 2,400 days. (b) 6.6 years. (c) No, it does not disappear at once.**
(a) Time = distance ÷ velocity = 600 m ÷ 0.25 m/day = 2,400 days.
(b) 2,400 days ÷ 365 days/year = 6.58 years, which rounds to 6.6 years.
(c) The gasoline already dissolved in or sorbed to the aquifer stays there. Groundwater mixes little, has little oxygen for microbes, and moves slowly, so the plume keeps drifting toward the well for years.
:::

:::sol SG-C9-E003
**(a) 20. (b) 4.2 mg/L. (c) 84 mg/L. (d) 42 times.**
(a) Dilution factor = bottle volume ÷ sample volume = 300 mL ÷ 15 mL = 20.
(b) DO drop = 8.6 − 4.4 = 4.2 mg/L.
(c) BOD₅ = 4.2 mg/L × 20 = 84 mg/L.
(d) 84 ÷ 2 = 42 times greater.
:::

:::sol SG-C9-E004
**(a) 48% upstream and 4.0% below the outfall. (b) 94%. (c) Below the outfall. (d) A DO or BOD₅ measurement.**
(a) Upstream total = 48 + 30 + 22 = 100, so 48 ÷ 100 × 100 = 48%. Below the outfall total = 3 + 12 + 60 = 75, so 3 ÷ 75 × 100 = 4.0%.
(b) Percent decrease = (48 − 3) ÷ 48 × 100 = 93.75%, which rounds to 94%.
(c) The site below the outfall has the lower DO. Sensitive animals, which need oxygen-rich water, nearly disappear there, while tolerant animals such as sludge worms dominate, which indicates organic waste and oxygen use by decomposers.
(d) Measure DO (expect lower, below about 5 mg/L, below the outfall) or BOD₅ (expect higher below the outfall).
:::

:::sol SG-C9-E005
**(a) 0.6 mg/L. (b) 50 kg per day. (c) To protect the water from microbes in the pipes.**
(a) Residual = dose − demand = 2.5 − 1.9 = 0.6 mg/L.
(b) 1 mg/L = 1 g/m³, so 2.5 g/m³ × 20,000 m³/day = 50,000 g/day = 50 kg/day.
(c) Dosing only the demand would leave no free chlorine. A residual keeps killing microbes that enter or regrow in the distribution pipes and storage on the way to the tap.
:::

:::sol SG-C9-E006
**(a) 40,000 m³. (b) 60,000 m³. (c) 5.8 × 10⁴ mg/L. (d) 1.7 times.**
(a) Fresh water = 100,000 × 0.40 = 40,000 m³/day.
(b) Brine = 100,000 − 40,000 = 60,000 m³/day.
(c) Salt in = 35,000 mg/L × 100,000 m³ = 35 kg/m³ × 100,000 m³ = 3.5 × 10⁶ kg. Brine concentration = 3.5 × 10⁶ kg ÷ 60,000 m³ = 58.3 kg/m³ = 58,333 mg/L ≈ 5.8 × 10⁴ mg/L.
(d) 58,333 ÷ 35,000 = 1.67, about 1.7 times saltier. The dense, salty brine can raise salinity near the outfall and stress or kill organisms that cannot tolerate it.
:::

:::sol SG-C9-E007
**(a) 700 L per day. (b) 28 m². (c) 4.3 days.**
(a) Flow = 5 people × 140 L/person/day = 700 L/day.
(b) Area = 700 L/day ÷ 25 L/(m²·day) = 28 m².
(c) Time = tank volume ÷ daily flow = 3,000 L ÷ 700 L/day = 4.29 days, which rounds to 4.3 days.
:::
