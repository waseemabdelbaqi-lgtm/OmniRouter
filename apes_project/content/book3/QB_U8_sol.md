## Unit 8 — Aquatic and Terrestrial Pollution

### Section A — MCQ Answers and Explanations

:::sol QB-U8-MCQ001
**Answer: C.** Pollution that is diffused over a wide area and has no single identifiable source, such as agricultural runoff, is a nonpoint source. Options A, B and D each describe one identifiable pipe or tank, so they are point sources; D is the most tempting because it is "leaking," but a single tank is still a single, identifiable source.
:::

:::sol QB-U8-MCQ002
**Answer: B.** Endocrine disruptors are chemicals that interfere with the endocrine (hormone) system of animals and can cause developmental problems and gender imbalances. A describes pathogens, C describes low-oxygen conditions, and D describes the effect of an oil spill.
:::

:::sol QB-U8-MCQ003
**Answer: A.** Mangroves and wetlands provide flood protection, including buffering coasts against storm surge and wave energy, so clearing them removes that protection for nearby communities. B, C and D describe functions that mangroves do not perform; the other genuine services (habitat, water filtration) are not what the question asks about.
:::

:::sol QB-U8-MCQ004

:::

:::sol QB-U8-MCQ005
**Answer: B.** POPs are synthetic, carbon-based molecules that resist breakdown, so they persist and can accumulate. A is wrong because POPs are synthetic, not naturally occurring elements; C and D describe fates that would remove the pollutant, the opposite of persistence.
:::

:::sol QB-U8-MCQ007
**Answer: D.** LD50 is the dose of a chemical that is lethal to 50% of the population of a particular species. A confuses lethality with sublethal symptoms, and B and C describe tissue concentration and half-life, which are different ideas.

:::sol QB-U8-MCQ008

:::sol QB-U8-MCQ009

:::sol QB-U8-MCQ030
**Answer: D.** Below the outfall, decomposers consume oxygen faster than it is replaced by exchange with air, so DO falls. Further downstream, much of the waste has been broken down, consumption slows, and reoxygenation exceeds consumption, so DO recovers. A is incorrect because the waste is most concentrated near the outfall, not at the sag point.

:::sol QB-U8-MCQ031

### Section B — FRQ Solutions

:::sol QB-U8-FRQ001
(a) The problem is eutrophication (nutrient enrichment leading to algal blooms and hypoxia/fish kills). *(1 point: identifies eutrophication, algal bloom or nutrient pollution; "fish kill" alone is not enough.)*

(b) Example: If runoff from the lawns of the housing development carries extra nitrogen and phosphorus into the pond, then nutrient concentrations and algal growth will be higher at the site receiving Inlet 1 than at the site receiving Inlet 2. *(1 point: if-then, testable and links fertilizer runoff to nutrients or algae.)*

(c) Independent variable: the source of the water (site/inlet, that is, residential runoff versus forested runoff) or the concentration of nutrients in the runoff. Dependent variable: algal abundance (e.g., chlorophyll or algal cell count) and/or nutrient concentration (nitrate, phosphate) in the water. *(1 point independent, 1 point dependent. Accept dissolved oxygen as a dependent variable.)*

(d) Collect water samples from S1, S2, S3 and S4 at the same time of day, several times through the season (for example weekly from May through August and after storms), with replicate samples at each site. Measure nitrate and/or phosphate, chlorophyll-a (or algal counts) and dissolved oxygen. Keep constant or record: depth of sampling, time of day, weather/rainfall, temperature, sampling method. *(3 points: 1 for sampling at all four sites with timing and repeated or replicated samples; 1 for a valid nutrient, algae or DO measurement; 1 for a variable kept constant or recorded.)*

(e) Nutrient and chlorophyll concentrations would be highest at S1 and also high at S3 and S4 downstream, while S2 would have low values. *(1 point: S1 higher than S2 with consistent downstream pattern.)*

(f) Excess nutrients cause an algal bloom. When the algae die, microbes decompose them and use up dissolved oxygen, causing hypoxia that kills fish. *(1 point: dead algae decomposed by microbes lowers DO. Must link decomposition to oxygen loss.)*

(g) Examples: plant a vegetated buffer or build a retention basin or rain garden to trap runoff; reduce or stop fertilizer use or use slow-release or phosphorus-free fertilizer. Justification: vegetation and basins absorb nutrients and slow runoff, so less nitrogen and phosphorus reach the stream. *(1 point: a valid action with a justification.)*
:::

:::sol QB-U8-FRQ002
(a) Mercury concentration increases at each successive trophic level, from 0.02 mg/kg in plankton to 3.6 mg/kg in loons. *(1 point.)*

(b) 0.90 mg/kg ÷ 0.02 mg/kg = 45 times greater. *(1 point for 45 with work shown.)*

(c) Mercury (as methylmercury) is absorbed and stored in the tissues of organisms (bioaccumulation) and is not easily excreted. Each predator eats many prey containing mercury, so the concentration per unit of body tissue increases at successively higher trophic levels (biomagnification). *(1 point for stored in tissue/persists; 1 point for predators consuming many contaminated prey so concentration rises up the chain.)*

(d) Burning coal at the power plant releases mercury into the air, and it is deposited on the lake and watershed. Bacteria in the water convert elemental mercury to highly toxic methylmercury, which organisms absorb and which accumulates. *(1 point: coal burning or other mercury-releasing activity such as mining; 1 point: bacteria convert it to methylmercury.)*

(e) Claim: pike from Lake Alder are safe to eat but pike from Lake Birch are not. Justification: Alder pike contain 0.20 mg/kg, below the 0.5 mg/kg limit, while Birch pike contain 0.90 mg/kg, nearly double the limit. *(1 point for a correct claim; 1 point for supporting data, with both values compared to 0.5.)*

(f) Reduce emissions from the power plant (install scrubbers, switch to renewable energy or natural gas); drawback: scrubbers or switching sources cost money and results take years because mercury already in sediments continues to cycle. Accept: stop mercury mining/fish consumption advisories with a drawback such as not fixing the cause. *(1 point: valid solution; 1 point: a reasonable drawback.)*
:::

:::sol QB-U8-FRQ003
(a) 250,000 people × 1.8 kg/person/day × 365 days/yr = 1.6425 × 10⁸ kg/yr ÷ 1,000 kg/t = **164,250 metric tons per year** (≈ 1.6 × 10⁵ t). *(1 point for correct set-up with units; 1 point for the correct answer.)*

(b) Landfilled fraction = 100% − 22% − 12% = 66%. 164,250 t × 0.66 = **108,405 t/yr**. *(1 point.)*

(c) 108,405 t × 1,000 kg/t = 1.08405 × 10⁸ kg; ÷ 600 kg/m³ = **180,675 m³/yr** (≈ 1.8 × 10⁵ m³). *(1 point for converting tons to kg and dividing by density; 1 point for answer.)*

(d) 4.0 × 10⁶ m³ ÷ 180,675 m³/yr = **≈ 22 years** (22.1). *(1 point.)*

(e) Landfilled fraction = 50%. 164,250 t × 0.50 = 82,125 t = 8.2125 × 10⁷ kg ÷ 600 kg/m³ = 136,875 m³/yr. Years = 4.0 × 10⁶ ÷ 136,875 = **≈ 29 years** (7 years longer). *(1 point for volume per year; 1 point for years.)*

(f) Drawbacks of composting: odors and attraction of rodents, plus land and labor costs. Further proposal examples: incineration or waste-to-energy (reduces volume, but releases air pollutants); capturing landfill methane for electricity (reduces landfill gas emissions and generates power); reduce waste at the source (reusable packaging, a pay-as-you-throw program). Justification must connect the proposal to less waste landfilled or a lower environmental impact. *(1 point: drawback; 1 point: valid proposal with justification.)*
:::

:::sol QB-U8-FRQ004
(a) Mortality increases as the dose increases for both insecticides, following an S-shaped curve that approaches 100% at high doses. Insecticide N's curve is shifted to the left of M's (N kills a greater percentage at every dose). *(1 point: positive relationship/sigmoid; 1 point: N and M differ in position or in mortality at each dose.)*

(b) N: between 0.1 µg (30%) and 0.3 µg (72%), so LD50 ≈ 0.17 µg per bee (accept 0.15–0.20). M: between 0.3 µg (38%) and 1 µg (74%), so LD50 ≈ 0.45 µg per bee (accept 0.4–0.5). *(1 point each.)*

(c) Insecticide N is more toxic, because a smaller dose (about 0.17 µg per bee versus about 0.45 µg) kills half the bees; for instance, at 0.3 µg N kills 72% and M kills 38%. *(1 point: N with supporting data.)*

(d) 72% × 40,000 = 0.72 × 40,000 = **28,800 bees**. *(1 point.)*

(e) Any two: LD50 measures only death, not sublethal effects (impaired navigation, reduced reproduction, weakened immunity); it is measured over a short time (24 h) and a single dose, so chronic or repeated exposure may differ; it may not predict the effect on other species or on bees exposed to mixtures; real exposure depends on how much insecticide bees actually encounter. *(1 point for each valid reason, maximum 2.)*

(f) Examples: spray at night or when bees are not foraging; avoid spraying flowering plants; use integrated pest management, targeted application, or a less toxic insecticide; leave buffer strips. Justification: reduces the dose of insecticide that bees contact, so fewer bees are killed. *(1 point: valid practice; 1 point: justification linked to lower exposure.)*
:::
