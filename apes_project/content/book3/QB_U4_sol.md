## Unit 4 — Earth Systems and Resources

### Section A — MCQ Answers and Explanations

:::sol QB-U4-MCQ001
**Answer: D.** Subduction carries water-rich oceanic crust into the mantle, where melting produces magma that rises to build volcanoes on the overriding plate; when the overriding plate is oceanic, the volcanoes form an island arc. A rift valley and a mid-ocean ridge are features of divergent boundaries, where plates move apart, and a transform fault forms where plates slide past each other. The tempting rift-valley option confuses the collision with a divergent setting.
:::

:::sol QB-U4-MCQ002
**Answer: D.** A locked fault resists motion while the plates continue to move, so elastic strain accumulates. When the stress overcomes the friction holding the fault, the rocks suddenly slip and release the stored energy as seismic waves. Plates do not stop moving, and smooth continuous slip releases energy gradually instead of producing a sudden earthquake. Volcanic activity can cause small tremors but is not the cause of fault earthquakes.
:::

:::sol QB-U4-MCQ003
**Answer: A.** Parent material is the original rock or sediment (for example bedrock, glacial deposits, or river-deposited material) that is weathered, and sometimes transported and deposited, to supply the mineral part of a soil. The surface layer of humus is organic matter produced by decomposition, not parent material. Dissolved nutrients are products of weathering and decay, and unweathered bedrock alone is only one possible source, since transported sediment also serves as parent material.
:::

:::sol QB-U4-MCQ004
**Answer: C.** Soil formation is faster where warm temperatures and abundant water speed chemical weathering of parent material and the growth and decomposition of organisms that add organic matter. Cold or dry conditions slow these processes. The cold, wet site has water but low temperatures and long freezing periods that slow chemical reactions and biological activity, so it forms soil more slowly than the warm, humid site.
:::

:::sol QB-U4-MCQ005
**Answer: B.** As water percolates, soil particles trap suspended material, clay and organic matter adsorb many contaminants, and microbes break down some of them, which cleans the water. The ability is limited: soils can be overwhelmed by large pollutant loads, and they do not stop water from recharging groundwater. More runoff would carry pollutants to streams instead of filtering them.
:::

:::sol QB-U4-MCQ006
**Answer: A.** Permeability is the rate at which water moves through a soil, and it depends on the size and connectedness of the pores. Sand has the largest particles and therefore the largest pores, so water drains quickly. Clay has very small pores that slow water movement even though they can hold a lot of water, silt is intermediate, and loam, a blend of all three, drains more slowly than sand.
:::

:::sol QB-U4-MCQ007
**Answer: B.** Dry air is about 78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen, 0.93% argon, and 0.04% carbon dioxide, so the percentages in order identify W, X, Y, and Z. Reversing nitrogen and oxygen is the most tempting error because oxygen is the gas people associate with air, but it is the second most abundant. Carbon dioxide, although important for climate, is a trace gas far less abundant than argon.
:::

:::sol QB-U4-MCQ008
**Answer: D.** The layers are defined by temperature gradients: temperature falls with altitude in the troposphere, rises in the stratosphere, falls again in the mesosphere, and rises in the thermosphere. The proportions of nitrogen and oxygen are nearly constant in the lower layers, so composition does not define them. Air pressure decreases gradually and never abruptly reaches zero, and water vapor is concentrated mainly in the troposphere but does not set the boundaries.
:::

:::sol QB-U4-MCQ009
**Answer: A.** Because Earth rotates, the surface moves at different speeds at different latitudes, and moving air appears to be deflected to the right in the Northern Hemisphere and to the left in the Southern Hemisphere. Differences in land and ocean heating cause local pressure differences, and the tilt of the axis causes the seasons, but neither deflects moving air in this way.
:::

:::sol QB-U4-MCQ010
**Answer: B.** A divide is the line of higher ground along which precipitation drains to one watershed on one side and to a different watershed on the other. A floodplain is flat land next to a stream that floods, a delta is sediment deposited where a river enters a larger body of water, and a recharge zone is an area where water enters an aquifer.
:::

:::sol QB-U4-MCQ011
**Answer: C.** The tilt of Earth's axis (about 23.5°) points the Northern Hemisphere toward the Sun for part of the orbit, which makes the Sun's rays more direct and lengthens daylight, so insolation increases. Earth is actually closest to the Sun in early January, so distance does not explain the seasons. The Sun's output is nearly constant over a year, and rotation speed does not change with season.
:::

:::sol QB-U4-MCQ012

Mountains do not absorb water vapor, shade does not explain the pattern, and winds continue over the crest rather than reversing.
:::

:::sol QB-U4-MCQ013

El Niño is a Pacific phenomenon, and the warm water shifts toward the east rather than staying only in the western Pacific.

A vertical plume or a vertical transform fault would not produce a steady increase of focal depth with horizontal distance, and ridge push at a divergent boundary produces only shallow earthquakes.

:::sol QB-U4-MCQ018

Clay particles are the smallest of the soil particles, so clay has many very small pores that hold water tightly and offer high resistance to flow. The statement that clay particles are larger than sand is false, and clay actually has the greater water-holding capacity.

:::sol QB-U4-MCQ023

Rising air at the equator (W) and near 60° (Y) cools, condenses, and produces rain. Cold air at the surface and ocean winds do not explain the dryness at X.

:::sol QB-U4-MCQ024
**Answer: B.** Between the equator and about 30° N, surface air flows toward the equator (south). Earth's rotation deflects this moving air to the right in the Northern Hemisphere, so it moves toward the southwest, which means the wind blows from the northeast. These are the northeast trade winds. Winds from the southeast occur in the Southern Hemisphere, where deflection is to the left.

:::sol QB-U4-MCQ025

Warm, moist air is less dense and rises; as it rises it cools, water vapor condenses, and heavy rain falls. Sinking air is dry, the Coriolis effect is weakest (zero) at the equator, and mountains do not circle the equator.
