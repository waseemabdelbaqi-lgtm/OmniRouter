## Chapter 1 — The Earth

:::sol SG-C1-E001
**Answers: (a) 3 half-lives; (b) 17,190 years; (c) 2.50 billion years; (d) too little carbon-14 would remain.**
(a) 100% → 50% → 25% → 12.5%, so n = 3. (b) Age = n × half-life = 3 × 5,730 yr = 17,190 yr. (c) 25% remaining is 2 half-lives, so age = 2 × 1.25 billion yr = 2.50 billion yr. (d) 100 million years is about 17,000 carbon-14 half-lives (100,000,000 ÷ 5,730 ≈ 17,450), so essentially no carbon-14 is left to measure; an isotope with a much longer half-life, such as potassium-40 or uranium-238, is needed.
:::

:::sol SG-C1-E002
**Answers: (a) 25 s; (b) 210 km; (c) 35 s; (d) one station gives only a distance, so three circles are needed to fix a point.**
(a) S–P interval = 08:14:35 − 08:14:10 = 25 s. (b) Distance = (S–P) × vP·vS ÷ (vP − vS) = 25 s × (6.0 × 3.5) ÷ (6.0 − 3.5) = 25 × 21.0 ÷ 2.5 = 25 × 8.4 = 210 km. (c) P travel time = 210 km ÷ 6.0 km/s = 35 s (check: S takes 210 ÷ 3.5 = 60 s, and 60 − 35 = 25 s). (d) One station gives only the distance, a circle of possible epicenters. Two circles cross at two points; a third circle picks the one point where all three meet.
:::

:::sol SG-C1-E003
**Answers: (a) 100 times; (b) 1,024 times; (c) intensity.**
(a) ΔM = 8.1 − 6.1 = 2.0, so amplitude ratio = 10² = 100. (b) Energy ratio ≈ 32² = 1,024, about 1,000 times more energy. (c) Modified Mercalli intensity differs between towns, because it measures shaking and damage at one place, which falls with distance from the epicenter and rises on soft ground. Magnitude is a single value for the earthquake's energy at the source.
:::

:::sol SG-C1-E004
**Answers: (a) 198 m/s, 713 km/h; (b) 3.5 h; (c) 9.9 m/s; (d) energy is squeezed into shallower, shorter waves.**
(a) v = √(g·d) = √(9.8 × 4,000) = √39,200 = 198 m/s; 198 × 3.6 = 713 km/h. (b) Distance = 2,500 km = 2,500,000 m; t = 2,500,000 ÷ 198 = 12,626 s; 12,626 ÷ 3,600 = 3.5 h. (c) v = √(9.8 × 10) = √98 = 9.9 m/s (about 36 km/h). (d) As the wave slows in shallow water its wavelength shortens and its energy is concentrated into a smaller volume of water, so the height increases (shoaling).
:::

:::sol SG-C1-E005
**Answers: (a) 9 min; (b) 75 min; (c) the lahar; (d) rhyolitic magma is viscous and traps gas.**
(a) t = 18 km ÷ 120 km/h = 0.15 h = 9 min. (b) t = 45 km ÷ 36 km/h = 1.25 h = 75 min. (c) The lahar allows far more time (75 min against 9 min). Monitoring that gives warning includes seismometers detecting swarms of small quakes, rain gauges and acoustic flow monitors in the valley. (d) Rhyolitic magma has high silica, so it is very viscous and traps dissolved gas until pressure builds and it explodes; low-silica basaltic magma is runny and lets gas escape quietly.
:::

:::sol SG-C1-E006
**Answers: (a) 2 h, Station 2 is ahead; (b) about 2,050 km; (c) 1,610 km; (d) meridians converge toward the poles.**
(a) Longitude difference = 100° − 70° = 30°; 30° ÷ 15° per hour = 2 h. Station 2 (70° W) is farther east, so it is 2 h ahead. (b) 1° of longitude at 52° N = 111 × 0.616 = 68.4 km; 30° × 68.4 = 2,051 km ≈ 2,050 km. (c) 66.5° − 52° = 14.5°; 14.5 × 111 km = 1,609.5 km ≈ 1,610 km. (d) Meridians run pole to pole and meet at the poles, so the distance between them shrinks as latitude increases (factor cos 52° = 0.616 at 52° N, 1 at the equator).
:::

:::sol SG-C1-E007
**Answers: (a) 2,000 m; (b) 1,200 m; (c) lithification (compaction and cementation); (d) metamorphic; marble.**
(a) 0.40 mm/yr × 5,000,000 yr = 2,000,000 mm = 2,000 m. (b) Compaction removes 40%, leaving 60%: 2,000 m × 0.60 = 1,200 m. (c) Compaction and cementation (lithification). (d) Heat and pressure without melting produce a metamorphic rock; limestone becomes marble.
:::
