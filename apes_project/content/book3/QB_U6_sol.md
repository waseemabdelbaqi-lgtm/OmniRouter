## Unit 6 — Energy Resources and Consumption

### Section A — MCQ Answers and Explanations

:::sol QB-U6-MCQ001
**Answer: A.** Renewable sources are replenished naturally at or near the rate of consumption, which describes wind. Uranium and natural gas exist in fixed amounts that cannot be easily replaced, so discovering new deposits does not make gas renewable. The carbon dioxide released by burning biomass does not decide whether it is renewable; replenishment rate does.
:::

:::sol QB-U6-MCQ002
**Answer: A.** Coal, oil, and natural gas together are the most widely used energy sources globally. Hydroelectric, nuclear, wind, and solar are all growing in some places but each supplies a much smaller share of global energy than fossil fuels.
:::

:::sol QB-U6-MCQ003
**Answer: B.** Accessibility and low cost explain the heavy use of wood in developing countries. Wood has a lower energy content per kilogram than coal or oil, burning it indoors produces particulates and carbon monoxide, and overharvesting can cause deforestation, so it is not always replenished.
:::

:::sol QB-U6-MCQ004
**Answer: B.** Natural gas is mostly methane (CH₄). Bitumen is the thick, tar-like component of tar sands, ethanol is a biofuel made by fermentation, and carbon dioxide is a product of combustion rather than a fuel.
:::

:::sol QB-U6-MCQ005
**Answer: D.** Greater heat, pressure, and depth of burial produce higher-quality coal. Lignite is the lowest grade, bituminous is intermediate, and anthracite is the highest grade with the most carbon and energy per kilogram.
:::

:::sol QB-U6-MCQ006
**Answer: C.** Fossil-fuel plants burn fuel to make steam; the steam turns a turbine, which spins a generator. Splitting nuclei describes fission in a nuclear plant, and combining hydrogen with oxygen to make electricity describes a fuel cell. A generator cannot be spun directly by heat.
:::

:::sol QB-U6-MCQ007
**Answer: D.** Commercial reactors use fission: a neutron splits a U-235 nucleus, releasing a large amount of heat. Burning or chemically oxidizing uranium is not the source of the energy, and fusion of hydrogen nuclei is not used in commercial power plants.
:::

:::sol QB-U6-MCQ008
**Answer: D.** Passive systems absorb heat directly from the sun without mechanical or electrical equipment. Active systems use pumps or fans to move a heated liquid or air, and photovoltaic cells convert light directly to electricity. Geothermal systems use heat from Earth’s interior, not sunlight.
:::

:::sol QB-U6-MCQ009
**Answer: B.** Dams flood land upstream and change river flow, temperature, and sediment downstream, so habitats are lost or changed. Hydroelectric plants do not burn fuel, so they do not produce air pollution or waste; sulfur dioxide, radioactive waste, and hydrogen sulfide are associated with coal, nuclear, and geothermal plants respectively.
:::

:::sol QB-U6-MCQ010
**Answer: D.** Wind turbines capture the kinetic energy of moving air, which spins the blades and the generator to produce electricity. The other sequences either invent chemical or thermal energy conversions that do not occur or run the process in reverse.
:::

:::sol QB-U6-MCQ011
**Answer: D.** Lowering the thermostat reduces the energy used for heating. Raising the water-heater temperature, leaving lights on, and running partly filled dishwashers all increase energy use.
:::

:::sol QB-U6-MCQ012
**Answer: A.** In a fuel cell, hydrogen combines with oxygen from the air to form water and release electrical energy, so water is the emission. Carbon dioxide, nitrogen oxides, and carbon monoxide are produced when carbon-containing fuels are burned.
:::

:::sol QB-U6-MCQ013
**Answer: C.** Steam and fluids from geothermal reservoirs can carry hydrogen sulfide, which smells like rotten eggs. Carbon monoxide has no odor, ozone is formed in the atmosphere from other pollutants, and radon is an odorless radioactive gas from rock.
:::

:::sol QB-U6-MCQ014
**Answer: A.** A renewable resource is replenished at or near the rate it is consumed. When use exceeds regrowth, the forest shrinks and deforestation results, so the fuel behaves as nonrenewable. Wood is not millions of years old like fossil fuels, and burning it does release CO₂.
:::

The middle-to-high change is only about one percentage point, hydroelectric exceeds wind and solar in the low- and middle-income groups, and biomass is largest in the low-income group.

:::sol QB-U6-MCQ020
**Answer: A.** The global distribution of coal, oil, and gas is not uniform and depends on regions’ geologic history; coal comes from buried ancient vegetation that was changed by heat and pressure. Modern population, current rainfall, and government choices do not control where coal formed.

:::sol QB-U6-MCQ021
**Answer: B.** Fracking can contaminate groundwater and release volatile organic compounds. Hydrogen sulfide and flooding are tied to geothermal and hydroelectric projects, radioactive waste and thermal pollution to nuclear plants, and bird and bat deaths to wind turbines.

:::sol QB-U6-MCQ022

:::sol QB-U6-MCQ024

:::sol QB-U6-MCQ025
**Answer: A.** Nuclear power does not produce air pollutants during fission and is considered cleaner for that reason, but it releases thermal pollution and radioactive solid waste. It does not burn fuel, and uranium exists in a fixed amount, so it is nonrenewable.
:::

:::sol QB-U6-MCQ026
**Answer: D.** Carbon in ethanol came from the atmosphere through photosynthesis, so burning it does not add additional carbon the way fossil fuels do. However, growing, harvesting, fermenting, and distilling the crop takes a lot of energy, giving a low EROI. Ethanol is made from plants, not from carbon buried for millions of years.
:::

:::sol QB-U6-MCQ029

:::sol QB-U6-MCQ030

It does not depend on sunlight, it is not used up like a fuel in the way described, and its main air emission concern is hydrogen sulfide rather than large amounts of CO₂.

:::sol QB-U6-MCQ031

### Section B — FRQ Solutions

:::sol QB-U6-FRQ001
(a) Contamination of groundwater (the aquifer) by methane, fracking fluid chemicals, or brine from the gas well. Release of volatile organic compounds to air is a related fracking concern but does not address drinking-water wells. *(1 point: groundwater/drinking-water contamination)*

(b) Example: *If* the gas well is fractured, *then* dissolved methane concentration will increase most in the monitoring well nearest the pad (M1) and by progressively smaller amounts in M2 and M3, compared with their baseline values. *(1 point: directional prediction that links distance from the pad, or before/after fracking, to methane concentration)*

(c) Collect water samples from M1, M2, and M3 several times (for example monthly for six months) **before** drilling to establish a baseline, then at the same schedule during and after fracking. Collect at least 3 replicate samples from each well each time, use the same sampling method and a certified laboratory, and analyze for dissolved methane (mg/L). *(1 point: sampling at all three wells before and after fracking at stated intervals; 1 point: replication/repeated measurements or equivalent reliability measure)*

(d) Independent variable: distance of the monitoring well from the pad (M1, M2, M3), or alternatively time relative to fracking (before vs. after). Dependent variable: concentration of dissolved methane in the water (mg/L). *(1 point each; the independent variable must match the hypothesis written in (b))*

(e) Any one: sampling depth within the aquifer; same laboratory method or detection limit; same season or time of day; same type of well and purge procedure. Keeping it constant means differences in methane can be attributed to distance or fracking rather than to the sampling method. *(1 point: valid controlled variable with a reason)*

(f) Support: methane in M1 rises after fracking compared with its baseline, the rise in M2 is smaller, and M3 shows little or no change. To decide whether the increase is real, compare the post-fracking values with the range of the baseline samples (for example, outside the baseline mean ± 2 standard deviations) or apply a statistical test; consider that natural methane can occur in aquifers, so the baseline matters. *(1 point: pattern with distance/time; 1 point: comparison with baseline variability or statistical test)*

(g) Any one with justification: install and pressure-test steel casing cemented through the aquifer so fluids and gas cannot leak into it (isolates the aquifer from the well); recycle or safely treat wastewater instead of using unlined pits (prevents spills); set minimum distances between wells and drinking-water supplies (adds a safety margin); continue monitoring wells (detects problems early). *(1 point: reasonable action that addresses groundwater contamination, with a justification)*
:::
