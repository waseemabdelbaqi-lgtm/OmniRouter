## Chapter 6 — Populations

:::sol SG-C6-E001
**Answer: (a) 70%; (b) 600 individuals; (c) 10% and 60%, so ages 40–60 lose more; (d) Type I, K-selected.**
(a) Percent surviving = 700 ÷ 1,000 × 100 = 70%.
(b) Deaths = survivors at 40 − survivors at 60 = 700 − 100 = 600 individuals.
(c) Ages 0–20: (1,000 − 900) ÷ 1,000 × 100 = 10%. Ages 40–60: (700 − 100) ÷ 1,000 × 100 = 60%. The 40–60 interval has the greater loss.
(d) Few deaths when young and most deaths late in life form a Type I curve. This fits a K-selected species: large body, few offspring, heavy parental care that keeps juvenile survival high.
:::

:::sol SG-C6-E002
**Answer: (a) 46 and 31.25 deaths per 1,000 live births; (b) about −32.1%; (c) see below.**
(a) IMR = infant deaths ÷ live births × 1,000. First year: 11,500 ÷ 250,000 × 1,000 = 46 per 1,000. Later year: 7,500 ÷ 240,000 × 1,000 = 31.25 per 1,000.
(b) Percent change = (31.25 − 46) ÷ 46 × 100 = −14.75 ÷ 46 × 100 = −32.07%, a decrease of about 32.1%.
(c) When infants usually survive, families no longer need many births to be sure that some children reach adulthood. With a lag, as parents see the change, they choose smaller families, so TFR falls.
