## Chapter 3 — Global Water Resources and Use

:::sol SG-C3-E001
**(a) 3.5 × 10⁷ km³; (b) 1.05 × 10⁷ km³; (c) 1.05 × 10⁵ km³, which is 0.0075% of all water.**
(a) Fresh water = 1.4 × 10⁹ km³ × 0.025 = 3.5 × 10⁷ km³.
(b) Groundwater = 3.5 × 10⁷ km³ × 0.30 = 1.05 × 10⁷ km³.
(c) Lakes and rivers = 3.5 × 10⁷ km³ × 0.003 = 1.05 × 10⁵ km³. As a share of all water: 1.05 × 10⁵ ÷ 1.4 × 10⁹ = 7.5 × 10⁻⁵ = 0.0075%. (Check: 0.3% of 2.5% = 0.0075%.)
:::

:::sol SG-C3-E002
**(a) about 9.6 °C; (b) about 47.6 °C; (c) the sand heats about five times as much as the water.**
Use ΔT = Q ÷ (m × c).
(a) Water: ΔT = 20,000 J ÷ (500 g × 4.18 J/(g·°C)) = 20,000 ÷ 2,090 = 9.6 °C.
(b) Sand: ΔT = 20,000 J ÷ (500 g × 0.84 J/(g·°C)) = 20,000 ÷ 420 = 47.6 °C.
(c) Water has a high specific heat because energy goes into breaking hydrogen bonds, so it warms little for the same energy input, while sand warms a lot. The sea therefore stays cooler than the beach.
:::

:::sol SG-U4-E015
**Answer: (a) 3.78 × 10⁷ m³. (b) 1.2 m³/s. (c) about 3,000 t (3,024 t). (d) about 0.19 mm per year.**

(a) Runoff volume = depth × area × C. Depth = 1,200 mm = 1.2 m; area = 90 km² = 9.0 × 10⁷ m². Volume = 1.2 m × 9.0 × 10⁷ m² × 0.35 = **3.78 × 10⁷ m³** per year.

(b) Discharge = volume ÷ time = 3.78 × 10⁷ m³ ÷ 3.15 × 10⁷ s = **1.2 m³/s**.

(c) Concentration: 80 mg/L × 1,000 L/m³ = 80,000 mg/m³. Mass = 80,000 mg/m³ × 3.78 × 10⁷ m³ = 3.02 × 10¹² mg. Divide by 10⁹ mg/t: **≈ 3,024 t**, or about 3.0 × 10³ metric tons of sediment per year.

(d) Cropland area = 0.15 × 90 km² = 13.5 km² = 1.35 × 10⁷ m². Volume of soil = mass ÷ bulk density = 3,024 t ÷ 1.2 t/m³ = 2,520 m³. Depth = volume ÷ area = 2,520 m³ ÷ 1.35 × 10⁷ m² = 1.87 × 10⁻⁴ m = **≈ 0.19 mm per year**.
:::

:::sol SG-U4-E007
**Answer: (a) 3.24 × 10⁷ m³. (b) 4.23 × 10⁷ m³. (c) About 31% increase. (d) Pavement reduces infiltration.**

Formula: runoff volume = precipitation depth × area × C. Convert 1 km² = 10⁶ m², so 120 km² = 1.2 × 10⁸ m².

(a) 0.90 m × 1.2 × 10⁸ m² × 0.30 = **3.24 × 10⁷ m³**.

(b) Paved part: 0.90 m × (20 × 10⁶ m²) × 0.85 = 1.53 × 10⁷ m³. Remaining part: 0.90 m × (100 × 10⁶ m²) × 0.30 = 2.70 × 10⁷ m³. Total = 1.53 × 10⁷ + 2.70 × 10⁷ = **4.23 × 10⁷ m³**.

(c) Percent increase = (new − old) ÷ old × 100 = (4.23 × 10⁷ − 3.24 × 10⁷) ÷ 3.24 × 10⁷ × 100 = 0.99 ÷ 3.24 × 100 ≈ **31%**.

(d) Pavement and rooftops are impervious, so rain cannot soak into the ground (infiltration drops) and nearly all of it runs off the surface. The characteristic that changed is the **vegetation or soil cover** (land surface type): natural plant cover and permeable soil were replaced by impervious surfaces. (The runoff coefficient, and so the speed of flow to the stream, increased.)
:::

:::sol SG-C3-E003
**(a) 3.0 × 10¹⁰ m³; (b) −1.2 × 10⁹ m³ per year; (c) 25 years.**
(a) Area = 5,000 km² × 10⁶ m²/km² = 5 × 10⁹ m². Stored = 5 × 10⁹ m² × 30 m × 0.20 = 3.0 × 10¹⁰ m³.
(b) Net = recharge − withdrawal = 3 × 10⁸ − 1.5 × 10⁹ = −1.2 × 10⁹ m³/yr (a loss).
(c) Years = 3.0 × 10¹⁰ ÷ 1.2 × 10⁹ = 25 years.
:::

:::sol SG-C3-E004
**(a) about 7.6 m; (b) about 89%; (c) floating ice already displaces its own weight of water.**
(a) Water from ice = 3.0 × 10⁶ km³ × 0.917 = 2.751 × 10⁶ km³. Rise = 2.751 × 10⁶ km³ ÷ 3.6 × 10⁸ km² = 7.64 × 10⁻³ km = 7.6 m.
(b) Fraction submerged = 917 ÷ 1,025 = 0.895, so about 89% (about 90%).
(c) Floating ice displaces a mass of water equal to its own mass, so when it melts the water it forms fills the space it displaced and the level hardly changes. Land ice is not displacing ocean water, so its meltwater is a net addition to the ocean.
:::

:::sol SG-C3-E005
**(a) 70 g; (b) 600 g; (c) evaporation exceeds precipitation in the subtropics.**
(a) Salt = 2,000 g × 35 g/1,000 g = 70 g.
(b) At 50 ppt, salt is 0.050 of the total mass: total = 70 g ÷ 0.050 = 1,400 g. Water evaporated = 2,000 − 1,400 = 600 g.
(c) Subtropical regions are hot and dry, so evaporation removes water but leaves salt behind, whereas heavy rain at the equator dilutes the surface water.
:::

:::sol SG-U5-E004
**Answer: (a) 342,000 m³. (b) 240,000 m³. (c) 102,000 m³ saved per year; a farmer might keep furrow because it is cheap.**

Water needed at the roots for the farm = 5,700 m³/ha × 40 ha = 228,000 m³.

(a) Furrow loses 1/3, so efficiency = 1 − 1/3 = 2/3. Water pumped = 228,000 ÷ (2/3) = **342,000 m³**.

(b) Drip loses 5%, so efficiency = 0.95. Water pumped = 228,000 ÷ 0.95 = **240,000 m³**.

(c) Savings = 342,000 − 240,000 = **102,000 m³ per year**. A farmer might keep furrow irrigation because it is inexpensive to build and run. Drip needs costly hoses, filters and maintenance, and it can clog. A small farm or a farm with low water prices may not recover the cost of switching. A common error is to multiply 228,000 by 1.33 and by 1.05, which gives a different answer; losses must be divided as shown.
:::

:::sol SG-U5-E013
**Answer: (a) −0.9 m/yr; 40 years. (b) −0.46 m/yr; still unsustainable. (c) Switch to drip irrigation, which is expensive.**

(a) Net change = recharge − withdrawal = 0.2 − 1.1 = **−0.9 m/yr**. Years until dry = 36 m ÷ 0.9 m/yr = **40 years**.

(b) Pumping after a 40% cut = 1.1 × 0.60 = 0.66 m/yr. New net = 0.2 − 0.66 = **−0.46 m/yr**. The water table is still falling because withdrawal (0.66) is greater than recharge (0.2), so use is **not sustainable**. The aquifer would last about 36 ÷ 0.46 ≈ 78 years, which is longer but still ends in depletion. Sustainable use needs pumping ≤ 0.2 m/yr, a cut of about 82%.

(c) Replace flood or furrow irrigation with drip or spray irrigation, which loses far less water to evaporation and runoff. Drip is costly to install and maintain and can clog. Other acceptable changes include planting less water-demanding crops (the drawback being lower income) and scheduling irrigation by soil moisture.
:::

:::sol SG-C3-E006
**(a) 3.5 × 10⁷ m³ (3.47 × 10⁷); (b) 4.1 × 10⁷ m³ (4.08 × 10⁷); (c) 6.1 × 10⁶ m³.**
(a) Use = 250,000 × 380 L/day × 365 days = 3.469 × 10¹⁰ L = 3.469 × 10⁷ m³ (1 m³ = 1,000 L).
(b) Customers receive 85% of production: produced = 3.469 × 10⁷ ÷ 0.85 = 4.081 × 10⁷ m³.

:::sol SG-C3-E007
**(a) about 4.4 × 10⁵ m³/day; (b) about 2.4 × 10⁵ m³/day; (c) about 64 ppt; (d) $70,000 per day.**
(a) Feed = product ÷ recovery = 200,000 ÷ 0.45 = 444,444 m³/day.
(b) Brine = feed − product = 444,444 − 200,000 = 244,444 m³/day.
(c) The product has no salt, so all salt leaves in the brine: salinity = 35 ppt × 444,444 ÷ 244,444 = 63.6 ppt (also 35 ÷ 0.55 = 63.6).
(d) Energy = 200,000 m³ × 3.5 kWh/m³ = 700,000 kWh. Cost = 700,000 × $0.10 = $70,000 per day.
:::
