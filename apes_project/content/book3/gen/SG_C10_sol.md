:::sol SG-U8-E009
**Answer:** (a) LD50 of X ≈ 23 mg/kg; LD50 of Y ≈ 191 mg/kg; (b) X is more toxic; (c) 0.58 mg; (d) below.

(a) Formula: LD50 ≈ low dose + [(50 − low response) ÷ (high response − low response)] × (high dose − low dose).

Insecticide X: 50% lies between 20 mg/kg (46%) and 40 mg/kg (72%). LD50 = 20 + [(50 − 46) ÷ (72 − 46)] × (40 − 20) = 20 + (4 ÷ 26) × 20 = 20 + 3.08 = 23.08, so **23 mg/kg**.

Insecticide Y: 50% lies between 200 mg/kg (52%) and 100 mg/kg (30%). LD50 = 100 + [(50 − 30) ÷ (52 − 30)] × (200 − 100) = 100 + (20 ÷ 22) × 100 = 100 + 90.9 = 190.9, so **191 mg/kg**.

(b) **Insecticide X is more toxic.** Its LD50 (23 mg/kg) is much lower than Y's (191 mg/kg), so a much smaller dose kills half the mice; X is about 8 times more toxic (191 ÷ 23 ≈ 8.3).

(c) Dose (mg) = LD50 × body mass = 23 mg/kg × 0.025 kg = 0.575 mg, or **about 0.58 mg**.

(d) LD50 measures only acute (24-hour) lethality in lab mice at one dose. It does not show chronic or sublethal effects (such as reproductive or hormonal harm), effects on fish or aquatic invertebrates, how the chemical behaves in water (persistence, bioaccumulation), or exposure at the low concentrations in a river. Any one of these is an acceptable limitation.
:::

:::sol SG-U8-E014
**Answer:** (a) log axis; (b) about 0.5 mg/L; (c) shows background mortality.

(a) The concentrations span seven orders of magnitude (0.01 to 100 mg/L), with each step ten times the one before. On a linear axis, the low doses would be crowded at the origin and the high ones would stretch far out, so the S-shaped response could not be seen. A log axis spaces each tenfold step equally and spreads the data into a readable S-curve. Set-up: put log₁₀ concentration on the x-axis (ticks at 0.01, 0.1, 1, 10, 100, equally spaced, i.e., exponents −2, −1, 0, 1, 2) and percent mortality (0–100%) on the y-axis; the control (0 mg/L) cannot be plotted on a log axis, so it is shown separately or noted beside the axis.

(b) The 50% mortality lies between 0.1 mg/L (24%, exponent −1) and 1 mg/L (61%, exponent 0). The fraction of the way = (50 − 24) ÷ (61 − 24) = 26 ÷ 37 = 0.70. The exponent = −1 + 0.70 = −0.30, so the concentration = 10⁻⁰·³⁰ ≈ 0.50, or **about 0.5 mg/L**.

(c) The control shows how many shrimp die from causes other than the metal (handling, temperature, age, starvation). Here 3% died with no metal, so some deaths at each concentration are not due to the metal, and the control gives the baseline against which the effect of the metal is judged. It also confirms that the dishes and conditions themselves were not harmful. (Correcting for the 3% would change the estimate only slightly, since the interpolated value stays near 0.5 mg/L.)
