### Section A — MCQ Answers and Explanations

:::sol CB-C5-MCQ001 | label=Question 1 | year=2010
**Official key: A.** Limestone is calcium carbonate built from the shells and skeletons of marine organisms, so seafloor sediment locks away carbon for geologic time. The keyed element is carbon; the other listed elements are not stored mainly as carbonate rock.
:::

:::sol CB-C5-MCQ002 | label=Question 2 | year=2016
**Official key: A.** A sink absorbs or stores more carbon than it releases. Buried marine sediment stores carbon for long periods, whereas deforestation, burning and decomposition release CO₂, and lawn nutrient uptake is not long-term storage.
:::

:::sol CB-C5-MCQ003 | label=Question 3 | year=2008
**Official key: C.** Nitrogen-fixing bacteria in soil and in root nodules convert atmospheric N₂ into ammonia and related compounds that plants can use, which is why plants colonizing poor soils can leave it enriched.
:::

:::sol CB-C5-MCQ004 | label=Question 4 | year=2010
**Official key: C.** Decomposers break dead matter into simple inorganic nutrients that producers can take up again. This recycling keeps nutrients cycling and is the essential service they provide.
:::

:::sol CB-C5-MCQ005 | label=Question 5 | year=2016

### Section B — FRQ Solutions

:::sol CB-C5-FRQ001 | label=Question 1 | year=2014
(a)(i) Photosynthesis: autotrophs take in CO₂ and build it into sugars and other organic molecules. *(1.)* (ii) Cellular respiration or decomposition converts organic carbon back to CO₂ (or CH₄ under anaerobic conditions) released to the air. *(1.)*

(b)(i) Two ocean sinks: CO₂ dissolves in seawater, where it forms carbonic acid and bicarbonate; marine organisms use carbon for shells and skeletons (calcium carbonate) that sink and become limestone; phytoplankton fix carbon and it sinks. *(2.)*
(ii) A long-term land sink: peat or wetland sediments, old-growth wood, soil organic matter, or carbonate rock. *(1.)*

(c) Other human activities: deforestation and burning biomass (removes sink and releases carbon); cement production, landfills and livestock produce CO₂ or methane. *(2.)*

(d) Problem: enhanced greenhouse effect causing global warming; consequence: sea-level rise and habitat loss, shifting climate zones, or ocean acidification dissolving shells. *(1 + 1.)*

(e)(i) Phosphorus has no significant atmospheric phase; it cycles slowly through rock, soil and water and is released by weathering. *(1.)* (ii) Needed for DNA, RNA, ATP and cell-membrane phospholipids (also bones and teeth). *(1.)*
:::

:::sol CB-C5-FRQ002 | label=Question 2 | year=2008
(a) The atmosphere (N₂ gas) is the main nitrogen reservoir. Cycling: nitrogen-fixing bacteria (soil and legume root nodules) and lightning convert N₂ to ammonia/ammonium; nitrifying bacteria oxidize this to nitrite and then nitrate; plants absorb nitrate/ammonium and build proteins and DNA; consumers obtain nitrogen by eating; decomposers return ammonium; denitrifying bacteria convert nitrate back to N₂. *(1 point for the atmosphere; up to 3 for the processes.)*

(b) The phosphorus cycle has no significant gas phase, its main reservoir is rock (sediments), and it depends far less on bacteria. *(1 point for any one.)*

(c) Nitrate (or ammonium) from fertilizer, or phosphate from detergents/fertilizer/manure. Human activity: fertilizer applied to fields washes off into streams. Problem: excess nutrients cause algal blooms and cultural eutrophication, with oxygen depletion and fish kills. *(1 point each for compound, activity, problem.)*

(d) Burning fossil fuels (or deforestation) adds CO₂ faster than sinks remove it. Consequences: enhanced greenhouse warming and rising sea level; ocean acidification; more extreme weather such as drought and stronger storms. *(1 point for the disruption; 1 point each for two consequences.)*
:::
