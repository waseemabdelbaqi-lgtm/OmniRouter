### Section B — FRQ Solutions

:::sol CB-C1-FRQ001 | label=Question 1 | year=2014
(a)(i) At a subduction zone, denser oceanic plate is forced beneath the other plate, often forming a deep trench. *(1.)*
(ii) The sinking slab heats and releases water, melting overlying mantle; the buoyant magma rises through the crust and erupts, building volcanic arcs and island chains. *(1 for melting, 1 for rising magma.)*

(b)(i) Primary succession is colonization of bare rock, ash or sand where no soil previously existed. *(1.)*
(ii) Pioneers such as lichens and mosses secrete acids and wedge into rock, weathering it into mineral particles; as they die and decompose, organic matter mixes with these particles, forming a thin soil that later plants can use. *(1 + 1.)*

(c)(i) A sudden vertical slip of the seafloor during a subduction earthquake displaces a huge volume of water, which radiates outward as waves growing taller in shallow water. *(1 + 1.)*
(ii) Tsunamis destroy coastal habitats (mangroves, reefs), drown land organisms, or contaminate soils with salt water. *(1.)*

(d)(i) At the moment of an earthquake, locked plates suddenly slip past each other horizontally, releasing stored energy. *(1.)*
(ii) Between quakes the plates stay stuck by friction while stress and strain accumulate. *(1.)*
:::

**(a)** Leaf litter is a protective, nutrient-recycling layer. It decomposes into humus and nutrients for plants, holds moisture, lowers erosion, shelters seedlings and decomposer organisms, and insulates the soil. One statement must relate directly to plants. *(2 points)*

**(b)** Three described changes (not just listed) if worms remove the litter in a year: soil moisture falls as evaporation rises; erosion and runoff increase (with sediment in streams); soil fertility and humus drop, and soil temperature swings widen. Also pH change or compaction. *(3 points)*

**(c)** Pick one change and tie it to invaders: with drier, less fertile, more exposed soil, generalist exotic plants with broad tolerance (such as stilt grass) out-compete natives adapted to moist, humus-rich conditions. *(1 point; must be based on a part (b) change)*

**(d)** Hypothesis: forest plots with worms will have thinner litter depth and lower soil moisture than worm-free plots. Procedure: set matched plots of equal area in the same forest; control plots without added worms, experimental plots with a stated number of worms; same period (for example one year); several replicate plots; control other variables (slope, canopy, rainfall). Data: measure the dependent variable quantitatively, such as litter mass or depth and soil moisture percentage, at set intervals; compare means statistically. *(1 hypothesis naming a measurable factor, 1 design with control, experimental and one specific (area/time/sample size), 1 elaboration such as replication, 1 quantitative data)*

:::sol CB-C1-FRQ004 | label=Question 4 | year=2016
**(a) Two climate factors and soil formation rate.** Temperature: warmth speeds weathering and microbial decomposition so soil forms faster, while cold slows biological activity (though freeze-thaw can crack rock). Precipitation: moderate to high rainfall increases weathering and biological activity; very heavy rain can erode soil and reduce formation, and very dry conditions slow it. Wind can add or remove particles. *(2 points: 1 each description, not just naming a factor)*

**(b) A horizon.** (i) Biotic: humus, roots, earthworms, fungi or bacteria. (ii) Abiotic: sand, silt, clay, water, air, minerals or nutrients. *(2 points)*

**(c)(i) Practice raising nitrates.** Heavy fertilizer application (or improperly sealed feedlots or manure lagoons). *(1 point)*

**(c)(ii) How.** Nitrate is soluble and not held by soil particles, so water percolating through the soil carries it down into groundwater (or runoff carries it to waters that recharge aquifers). *(1 point)*

**(d)(i) Effect of acid deposition on plants.** Acidic soil leaches away nutrient cations such as calcium and magnesium, releases toxic aluminum, damages roots and lowers the soil's buffering, so growth declines. *(1 point)*

**(d)(ii) Remediation.** Add crushed limestone (lime) to neutralize the acid. *(1 point)*

**(e) Two ways climate change degrades soil.** Hotter, drier conditions cause desertification; higher evaporation of irrigation water causes salinization; heavier rain increases erosion and leaching; warmer soil breaks down organic matter faster; sea level rise brings salt water to coastal soils. *(2 points: 1 each)*
:::
