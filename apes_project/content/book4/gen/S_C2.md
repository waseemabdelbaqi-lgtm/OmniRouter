### Section A — MCQ Answers and Explanations

:::sol CB-C2-MCQ001 | label=Question 1 | year=2008
**Official key: B.** The ozone layer sits in the stratosphere, where ozone absorbs ultraviolet radiation and warms the air, so temperature rises with altitude there. On the profile, this is the layer above the troposphere where temperature increases.
:::

:::sol CB-C2-MCQ002 | label=Question 2 | year=2008
**Official key: A.** Air density and pressure fall with height, so the lowest layer, the troposphere, holds roughly 75–80 percent of the atmosphere's mass and molecules.
:::

:::sol CB-C2-MCQ003 | label=Question 3 | year=2008
**Official key: A.** Weather arises from rising and sinking air, evaporation, condensation and clouds, all of which occur in the troposphere, the layer touching the surface.
:::

:::sol CB-C2-MCQ004 | label=Question 4 | year=2008
**Official key: E.** Air pressure decreases with altitude, so the outermost layer (exosphere) has the least pressure and is where the atmosphere thins into space.
:::

:::sol CB-C2-MCQ005 | label=Question 5 | year=2010
**Official key: D.** The stratosphere contains the ozone layer, the highest natural ozone concentration. Weather and most water vapor lie in the troposphere.
:::

:::sol CB-C2-MCQ007 | label=Question 7 | year=2010
**Official key: E.** Solar input, atmospheric gases, land-ocean distribution and Earth's rotation and orbit shape global climate. Seasonal temperature inversions are local weather phenomena.
:::

:::sol CB-C2-MCQ008 | label=Question 8 | year=2016
**Official key: A.** Surface currents driven by winds and Earth's rotation move warm water poleward and cold water toward the tropics, moderating climates. They do not cause lake turnover, restrict the jet stream or reduce ocean acidification.
:::

:::sol CB-C2-MCQ009 | label=Question 9 | year=2024
*(The source copy had no published key; this answer was derived by Success4Sure Academy.)* **Answer: D.** Air rises over the windward side, cools and drops its moisture, then descends dry on the leeward side. This creates a rain shadow and desert-like land downwind.
:::

:::sol CB-C2-MCQ010 | label=Question 10 | year=2024
*(The source copy had no published key; this answer was derived by Success4Sure Academy.)* **Answer: C.** West of the mountains gets much more rain than the east year-round, consistent with a rain shadow on the east side.
:::

:::sol CB-C2-MCQ011 | label=Question 11 | year=2016
**Official key: C.** In the Hadley cell warm air rises at the equator and drops moisture as rain, then flows poleward, cools and sinks near 30°. Sinking air warms and dries, giving low humidity and desert conditions.
:::

:::sol CB-C2-MCQ012 | label=Question 12 | year=2010
**Official key: C.** In 1998 the surface was very warm, indicating weakened upwelling that carries nutrients up, so productivity and catch fell.
:::

:::sol CB-C2-MCQ013 | label=Question 13 | year=2010
**Official key: B.** El Niño brings warm water to the Peruvian coast. It is a recurring warm-water event in the equatorial Pacific, unlike eutrophication or salinization.
:::

:::sol CB-C2-MCQ014 | label=Question 14 | year=2016
**Official key: A.** Fish-eating birds depend on a steady fish supply near their nests. When warm El Niño water reduces fish near the coast, adults cannot find enough food for chicks, and breeding success and numbers fall.
:::

:::sol CB-C2-MCQ015 | label=Question 15 | year=2024
*(The source copy had no published key; this answer was derived by Success4Sure Academy.)* **Answer: B.** El Niño is the warm phase of ENSO, a recurring shift in Pacific surface temperatures that changes weather patterns worldwide. Hadley cells, thermal inversions and rain shadows are fixed or local features and are not periodic Pacific phenomena.
:::
