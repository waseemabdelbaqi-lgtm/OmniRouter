:::sol CB-C4-MCQ008 | label=Question 8 | year=2016
**Official key: C.** Per unit area, open-ocean productivity is low because nutrients are scarce. The ocean covers about 70% of Earth's surface, though, so the total productivity summed over that huge area (mostly by microscopic phytoplankton) is very large. The area covered, not the size of the individual producers, drives the total.
:::

:::sol CB-C4-FRQ006 | label=Question 6 | year=2016
**(a) Why disease seldom causes extinction.** Any one valid reason: genetic variation means some individuals resist and survive to reproduce; hosts and pathogens co-evolve toward lower lethality; a pathogen that kills all hosts loses its own habitat; and deaths lower population density, so transmission slows. *(1 point: one correct explanation)*

**(b)(i) Two threats to a very small population.** Examples: hard to find mates when individuals are scarce or widely dispersed; low genetic diversity (inbreeding, a bottleneck, one disease able to hit nearly all individuals); competition for roosts or food from similar species; loss of group benefits such as shared warmth or protection; vulnerability to a disturbance such as a drought or fire. *(2 points: 1 each, first two only)*

**(b)(ii) Slow recovery.** Little brown bats have one or two pups a year, mature late and live long (long generation time), so population growth is slow even with high survival. Heavy parental care also limits number of offspring. *(1 point: one biological trait with explanation)*

**(c) Effects on other organisms.** Examples: more insects (bat prey) and more insect-borne disease or crop pests; fewer predators of bats because less food; fewer plants that bats pollinate or whose seeds they disperse; less guano feeding cave communities; less fungus (WNS) if bats are fewer; competitors with similar niches gain; trophic cascade. *(2 points: 1 each, first two only)*

**(d) Two forest ecosystem services.** Any two, each with a human benefit: timber and fuel for construction and paper; oxygen production for breathing; soil protection and water filtration giving clean drinking water and flood control; carbon sequestration slowing climate change; habitat and biodiversity supplying food, medicine; recreation and culture. *(2 points: 1 each service linked to a benefit)*

**(e) Increase in emerging human diseases.** A reason plus explanation, e.g. climate warming lets disease vectors such as mosquitoes survive in new areas; more global travel moves pathogens quickly between regions; greater contact with wildlife through habitat clearing exposes people to zoonotic pathogens; antibiotic resistance produces new strains; denser populations pass disease more easily. *(2 points: 1 reason, 1 correct explanation of how it raises disease)*
